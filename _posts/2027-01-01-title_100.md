To verify that the unequal flow split explanation is consistent with the experimental tracer data, we can break the analysis down into two steps: finding the actual flow split from the RTD moments, and calculating the resulting conversion.
------------------------------
## Step 1: Find the Flow Split from RTD Moments
Let the total volume be $V_{\text{total}} = 2V$ and the total flow rate be $v$. For parallel systems, the mean residence time ($\bar{t}$) is:
$$\bar{t} = \frac{MO_1}{MO_0} = \frac{120}{1} = 120\text{ s}$$ 
Since $\bar{t} = \frac{V_{\text{total}}}{v} = \frac{2V}{v} = 120\text{ s}$, the space time for an equal ideal split is $\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{V}{v} = 60\text{ s}$.
If the flow is maldistributed, let a fraction $\alpha$ go to Reactor 1 and $(1-\alpha)$ go to Reactor 2. The individual reactor residence times become:
$$\tau_1 = \frac{60}{\alpha} \quad \text{and} \quad \tau_2 = \frac{60}{1-\alpha}$$ 
The second raw moment ($M_2$) for two parallel CSTRs is given by the flow-weighted sum of their individual second moments ($2\tau_i^2$):
$$M_2 = \frac{MO_2}{MO_0} = \alpha(2\tau_1^2) + (1-\alpha)(2\tau_2^2)$$ 
$$4.501 \times 10^4 = 2 \left[ \alpha \left(\frac{60}{\alpha}\right)^2 + (1-\alpha)\left(\frac{60}{1-\alpha}\right)^2 \right] = 7200 \left[ \frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)} \right]$$ 
Solving for $\alpha(1-\alpha)$:
$$\alpha(1-\alpha) = \frac{7200}{45010} \approx 0.160$$ 
$$\alpha^2 - \alpha + 0.160 = 0 \implies \alpha = 0.2 \text{ or } 0.8$$ 
The tracer study confirms that the flow is severely maldistributed with an 80/20 split.
------------------------------
## Step 2: Verify the Resulting Conversion
First, determine the reaction rate constant ($k$) using the ideal design specifications (equal 50/50 split where $\tau = 120\text{ s}$ and conversion $X = 0.90$):
$$X_{\text{design}} = \frac{k\tau}{1 + k\tau} \implies 0.90 = \frac{120k}{1 + 120k}$$ 
$$0.90 + 108k = 120k \implies k = 0.075\text{ s}^{-1}$$ 
Now, compute the actual space times and conversions for each reactor under the 80/20 flow split ($\alpha = 0.2$):

* Reactor 1 ($\alpha = 0.2$):
$$\tau_1 = \frac{60}{0.2} = 300\text{ s} \implies X_1 = \frac{0.075(300)}{1 + 0.075(300)} = \frac{22.5}{23.5} \approx 0.9575$$ 
* Reactor 2 ($1-\alpha = 0.8$):
$$\tau_2 = \frac{60}{0.8} = 75\text{ s} \implies X_2 = \frac{0.075(75)}{1 + 0.075(75)} = \frac{5.625}{6.625} \approx 0.8491$$ 
* Overall Conversion ($X_{\text{total}}$):
$$X_{\text{total}} = \alpha X_1 + (1-\alpha) X_2$$ 
$$X_{\text{total}} = 0.2(0.9575) + 0.8(0.8491) = 0.1915 + 0.6793 = \mathbf{0.871}$$ 

## Conclusion
The calculated overall conversion of 0.87 exactly matches the observed under-performance. Therefore, the explanation that a faulty flow splitter causes an uneven 80/20 flow distribution is fully consistent with the experimental measurements.
